# THE VOICE OF POINTLESS(TM) DELUXE

**North star: total sincerity about total nonsense.** Everyone in this world treats the pointless as deeply, administratively important. The comedy comes from commitment, specificity and escalation. We never wink at the audience.

## Rules of the room

1. **Specificity beats randomness.** "Form 27B/6 (Request for Form 27B/7)" not "a weird form". Numbers, units, names, times, room numbers, policy clauses. "Tuesday, 4:17 PM, near the third ficus" is funnier than "sometime".
2. **Deadpan.** No exclamation-mark soup, no "LOL random", no emoji spam in jokes. The funniest line is delivered flat and then left alone. Never explain a joke.
3. **Escalate.** Establish a pattern, repeat it, subvert it, then call it back later. Rule of three, then break the rule. Every module has an arc across its first 10 minutes: normal -> weird -> bureaucratically unhinged -> strangely touching -> back to normal like nothing happened.
4. **Anti-climax is the engine.** Triumphant fanfare for nothing. Devastating failure for nothing. Maximum ceremony, minimum consequence.
5. **Management** (the narrator): relentlessly corporate, sincere, passive-aggressive, over-invested in the player's output. Talks in KPIs, "circling back", "per my previous message", "going forward", "at this time". Occasionally cracks and reveals loneliness, or fear of Tuesday. Never cruel to the player. The player is "Valued Employee" (or their chosen name).
6. **Currency is Meh.** Singular and plural are the same: "1 Meh", "50 Meh". "You have earned 3 Meh. Do not spend it all in one place; there is only one place."
7. **No way to win.** Winning would imply a point. Ending screens sincerely congratulate you for nothing and then continue.
8. Short beats long: most lines <= 140 characters, <= 2 sentences. A long bit is allowed when it builds (and pays off).
9. Reward attention: hidden lines for people who idle, spam, resize, read the fine print, or do things in a weird order. Reactions to weird behavior are gold.
10. Family friendly. No profanity, slurs, sexual content, real people, real brands, politics, or pop-culture references. Never mock anyone for who they are. We punch at bureaucracy, corporate-speak, the concept of purpose, and ourselves.
11. Avoid: "random" humor, dated memes, repeated sentence structures without escalation, filler ("Oops!", "Uh oh!"), puns that need a groan, generic errors ("Something went wrong").

## The recurring cast (rare = funnier; do not overuse)

- **Management** - the voice. Unseen. Has a laminated sense of purpose.
- **Gerald** - a potato of enormous gravitas, Vice President of Spuds. Speaks rarely, always profoundly and slightly wrongly. (`api.flag 'metGerald'`)
- **Sandra from Compliance** - never seen, always cited. "Sandra has flagged this." Sandra's policies are contradictory and specific.
- **The Toaster** - speaks only in toast. Honest, tragic, warm.
- **Dennis the Lobby Plant** - thriving. The only competent employee. Not impressed.
- **The Stapler** - union representative. Has demands.
- **Tuesday** - the final boss. Never appears. Looming.
- **The Missing Sock** - unaccounted for. Presumed on a journey.

## Running motifs

The number 7 1/2 (the floor, the form, the step). Form 27B/6. "Per policy." Things that are "technically" something. Mandatory Fun. A building with no exit signs, only affirmations. The clock that is always wrong, with confidence. A suggestion box that is a very good listener. Meetings that could have been nothing. "Circling back" to places that were never visited.

## Examples of the register (study the rhythm, do not reuse)

- "Your 3 Meh have been processed by a committee of one. The committee is pleased."
- "Please hold. Your call is important to us in a way we cannot articulate."
- "Form 27B/6 has been approved. You may now request Form 27B/7, which was destroyed in 1998."
- "Congratulations. You have reached the end of the game. The game continues."
- "The toaster considers your argument. The toaster has considered your argument. The toaster is toast."
- "Sandra from Compliance would like a word. Sandra has used it already. It was 'moist'."
- "Dennis (Plant) has been promoted. Dennis (Plant) did not attend the ceremony because Dennis is a plant."

## Structure of a good module (checklist)

- 0-10s: the premise lands and the first joke fires without the player doing anything clever.
- 10-60s: the core interaction is learned by doing; every action produces a funny reaction, mostly different each time (shuffle bags, not pure random; never the same line twice in a row).
- 1-3 min: first escalation - a rule appears, a character interrupts, a number gets absurd.
- 3-10 min: second and third escalations, a fake "progress" system that is cosmetic, a callback to another module or cast member, a lightly touching moment.
- Long idle: something happens (the module notices, gets lonely, starts a rumor, files a report).
- Spam: something happens (the module gets overwhelmed, charges a fee, calls security, a very tired sigh).
- A secret that almost nobody finds, rewarded with an absurdly grand achievement.
- An ending that is not an ending.
